Video 1 · The Lithuanian school system

How school works in Lithuania

Summary

  • School is free and compulsory until age 16. Teenagers usually go to a gymnasium. Gymnasium grades I–IV are the same as grades 9–12.
  • To enrol, parents contact the education department of their municipality. In Vilnius, you apply online and can choose up to 3 schools. A residence permit is not needed to start.
  • A school day: lessons are 45 minutes and usually start at 8:00. Grades go from 1 to 10. 4 is the lowest pass.
  • The e-diary (Tamo or eDienynas) shows grades, homework and messages from teachers. Parents have access too.
  • Adaptation period: new students can get up to one year of extra Lithuanian lessons, a personal study plan and help from a psychologist.
  • Exams: after gymnasium grade IV, students take state maturity exams. The Lithuanian exam is needed for the certificate.

Try this

In your first week, ask your class teacher (klasės auklėtojas) for a short meeting with your parents. Bring your questions and your school documents from Ukraine.

Video 2 · Challenges at school & language

Challenges at school, and how to beat them

Three situations

“I don't understand the task.”
Ask “Ar galite pakartoti?” (Can you repeat?). Use English while you learn Lithuanian. Ask for the task in writing, use a translator app and go to the extra Lithuanian lessons.
“I don't know anyone.”
Say labas first. Ask your class teacher for a buddy. Join a club after school, and look for other Ukrainian students.
“I feel stressed or homesick.”
Talk to someone you trust. The school psychologist is free. You can also call Vaikų linija, 116 111, a helpline for under-18s that also speaks English.

Try this

Learn one sentence today: “Nesuprantu, ar galite paaiškinti?” That means “I don't understand, can you explain?”

Video 3 · Integrating into Lithuanian life

Life outside school

Five tips

  1. Use Lithuanian every day: labas in the shop, ačiū on the bus.
  2. Find a club. In Vilnius, the city pays €25 a month (€50 for families with free school meals) for one after-school activity.
  3. Visit the library. A library card is free, and libraries have quiet study places and events.
  4. Learn the traditions: Kaziuko mugė in March, Joninės in June and Kūčios on Christmas Eve.
  5. Explore the country: Kaunas, Trakai and the sea in Klaipėda are only short trips away.

Try this

This week, choose one club or one library event, and go once. That's how it starts.

Four useful resources

We checked every link in November 2026.

For parents

Foreigners in Vilnius: Education

What you'll find: the official city guide for school applications, free school meals, help with school supplies and the €25 club voucher. It is in Ukrainian, English, Lithuanian and Russian.

How it helps: step-by-step instructions and phone numbers for real people who speak English or Russian.

Open foreigners.vilnius.lt

For students & parents

Lithuanian for Ukrainians (A1–A2)

What you'll find: a free online course from Vytautas Magnus University. It has 10 chapters with audio, comics and exercises, and the explanations are in Ukrainian.

How it helps: you can learn at home, at your own speed, and practise what you hear in your extra Lithuanian lessons.

Open the VDU course

For parents

Access to education in Lithuania

What you'll find: a clear overview from Pravovsim, a Danish Refugee Council platform. It explains free schooling, municipal coordinators, personal study plans and Ukrainian-language schools.

How it helps: it answers the big questions on one page before you visit the school.

Open pravovsim.org

For students

Helplines (UNHCR Lithuania)

What you'll find: free support lines. For example, Vaikų linija, 116 111, for anyone under 18, by phone or chat, in English and Lithuanian.

How it helps: when you feel sad, stressed or alone, you can talk to someone today, without your name.

Open help.unhcr.org
